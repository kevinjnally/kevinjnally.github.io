Structural System Optimization

The Hunting Park Residences project challenged our interdisciplinary senior design team to engineer a resilient, nine-story mixed-use building prioritizing low-embodied carbon materials. The final structural strategy required balancing the unique demands of a concrete podium with the lightweight efficiency of a mass timber superstructure.

To optimize the lateral system, we utilized cast-in-place concrete shear walls for the podium level, providing necessary stiffness at the base. For the levels above, we transitioned entirely to cross-laminated timber (CLT) shear walls. The podium floor system was designed with a 12-inch thick slab supported by T-shaped beams.

Figure 1: Lateral system layout highlighting the transition from concrete to CLT shear walls.

Load Path & Detailing

A critical phase of the project involved refining the load path to maximize efficiency and reduce unnecessary material costs. By re-evaluating the architectural layout, we successfully eliminated the need for transfer girders. Girder designs were fully recalculated based on regular gravity loads, streamlining the structural frame.

Extensive attention was given to connection detailing and column capacity. In detailing the concrete supports, the transverse reinforcement design was finalized assuming 8 No. 8 longitudinal bars, ensuring robust ductile behavior at key intersections.
